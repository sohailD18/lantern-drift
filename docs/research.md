# Lantern Drift: Reference Games Research

Three existing mobile games were analyzed for mechanics, visual style, and engagement. Each one influenced a different part of Lantern Drift: the mood, the controls, and the way danger is communicated.

## 1. Alto's Odyssey

- **Platform:** Mobile (Google Play / App Store). Store link: _(add the link)_
- **Core mechanic:** A one-touch endless sandboarding game. The player taps to jump and holds to backflip over chasms and obstacles, using slopes to build speed.
- **Visual style:** Minimalist dark silhouettes in the foreground over soft sunset and desert gradients, with weather effects that change the mood.
- **Why people keep playing:**
  - The control is one touch, so it is learned in seconds.
  - The flow is calm and soothing, which makes short sessions relaxing.
  - Hazards contrast clearly with safe ground, so deaths feel fair.
- **What I took for Lantern Drift:** the calm, painterly atmosphere, layered painted backgrounds with slow parallax, and a one-touch, touch-and-mouse-friendly control.
- **How Lantern Drift is different:** the player does not steer the character directly. They draw wind currents, and the lantern drifts along them.

## 2. Downwell

- **Platform:** Mobile and PC. Store link: _(add the link)_
- **Core mechanic:** A vertical falling arcade game. The player's boots fire downward, which slows the fall, destroys enemies below, and recharges on landing.
- **Visual style:** A strict high-contrast palette of black, white, and red, so enemies, gems, and ledges are readable at speed.
- **Why people keep playing:**
  - One button does two jobs (attack and slow down), which creates a constant risk-and-reward choice.
  - Runs are short and instantly restartable.
  - Everything on screen is readable at a glance.
- **What I took for Lantern Drift:** vertical movement through a tall level, one input with more than one effect (holding a finger still brakes the lantern), and strong readability, such as a red rim on thorns so they stay visible in the dark.
- **How Lantern Drift is different:** the goal is to climb toward a festival sky, not fall, and the player's light is both health and vision.

## 3. Archero

- **Platform:** Mobile (Google Play / App Store). Store link: _(add the link)_
- **Core mechanic:** A top-down action game in small rooms. The character attacks automatically while standing still, so the player alternates between moving to dodge and stopping to deal damage.
- **Visual style:** Clean, bright arena scenes, with red warning lines that telegraph enemy attacks before they land.
- **Why people keep playing:**
  - The controls are just a drag, with no separate attack button.
  - Short rooms give a steady sense of progress.
  - Telegraphed attacks make danger feel avoidable, not random.
- **What I took for Lantern Drift:** a "stop to act" trade-off (stopping brakes the lantern), clear warnings before danger (the pulsing Gust arrow before wind blows), and short, separate levels.
- **How Lantern Drift is different:** there is no combat. The player survives by managing light and drifting around hazards.

## Summary

| Game | Taught me | Used in Lantern Drift |
|---|---|---|
| Alto's Odyssey | Calm atmosphere and one-touch control | Painted backgrounds, parallax, soft glow, touch and mouse input |
| Downwell | Readability and one input with two effects | Red-rimmed hazards, hold-still braking, tall vertical levels |
| Archero | Telegraphed danger and short sessions | Gust warnings, three short levels with one new rule each |

## What makes Lantern Drift original

Lantern Drift combines the calm, painterly mood of atmospheric mobile games with simple controls, and adds two ideas of its own:

1. **Draw the wind.** The player never moves the lantern directly. They draw short wind trails with a finger or mouse, and the lantern is pushed along them, so it feels like drifting, not driving.
2. **Light is the whole system.** The lantern's glow is both health and vision. The world outside the glow is dark, fireflies grow the light, and hazards shrink it.

Each level adds one new rule: collecting fireflies (Meadow), thorns and gusts (Thorn Forest), and a chasing shadow with dormant lanterns that push it back (Storm Sky).
